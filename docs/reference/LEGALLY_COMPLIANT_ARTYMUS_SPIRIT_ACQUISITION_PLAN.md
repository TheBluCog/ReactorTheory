## Executive Summary

The plan concludes that Spirit should not be treated as a conventional public-stock M&A target. The viable path is a **court-supervised Section 363 selected-asset purchase** or near-equivalent plan-sponsor transaction negotiated with the secured creditor stack and approved by the Bankruptcy Court.

Minority-stock accumulation or creeping-control strategies are rejected because legacy equity is expected to be wiped out, operations had ceased, and any usable airline authority still requires transportation regulator review.

---

## Core Acquisition Recommendation

Recommended path:

```text
Section 363 selected-asset purchase
        OR
near-equivalent plan-sponsor transaction
```

The target asset package should be narrowly defined around transferable and usable operating assets:

- Brand and domains
- Reservation and customer systems where transferable
- Selected certificates and operating authorities subject to approval
- Slots, gates, and route authorities where assignable
- Spare parts and simulators
- Selected aircraft or leases
- Loyalty-program IP
- Only contracts that can be cured and assumed on acceptable economics

The buyer should avoid buying old public stock and should avoid assuming broad legacy liabilities unless specifically required and economically justified.

## Capital and Blockchain Findings

ARTYMUS blockchain-based assets or claims cannot be used as closing consideration unless they are:

1. independently audited,
2. legally characterized,
3. valued,
4. lien-checked,
5. and converted into something courts, lenders, creditors, and regulators will treat as hard capital.

The report emphasizes that ARTYMUS claims can support the story only after diligence converts them into bankable value.

They cannot substitute for:

- cash,
- committed equity,
- lender-funded liquidity,
- or court-acceptable closing consideration.

## Token Architecture Findings

### AADST

AADST should be structured as a tokenized security representing HoldCo common equity, preferred equity, or a security entitlement in that equity.

Required features:

- valid registration or exemption,
- Rule 506(c) for U.S. accredited investors,
- Regulation S for offshore investors,
- whitelisted wallets,
- transfer-agent recordkeeping,
- broker-dealer / ATS controls for secondary trading,
- restricted transfers,
- investor eligibility checks,
- KYC / AML / sanctions controls.

### SRT

SRT should be post-close loyalty and rewards infrastructure, not acquisition funding.

It may provide:

- travel value,
- loyalty benefits,
- advisory governance signals,
- customer preference signals,
- route-demand signals.

It must not provide:

- profit sharing,
- dividends,
- appreciation rights,
- residual asset rights,
- binding control over airline operations,
- investment-style marketing.

## Regulatory Architecture

The report identifies these primary regulatory layers:

- Bankruptcy Court approval for Section 363 transaction mechanics
- DOT economic authority and transfer / recommencement review
- FAA safety authority and operating readiness
- U.S. citizen control requirements
- HSR / antitrust review if thresholds are triggered
- Securities compliance for AADST
- Broker-dealer / ATS requirements for secondary trading
- Transfer-agent recordkeeping
- KYC / AML / sanctions / wallet screening
- Consumer protection and privacy controls for airline customer data

---

## Due Diligence Checklist

Before treating the transaction as execution-ready, ARTYMUS should demand:

- Full debt, lien, and collateral schedule
- DIP / exit / revolver / aircraft / slot / spare-parts encumbrances
- Claims register and schedule of proposed assumed and rejected contracts
- Aircraft-by-aircraft ownership, lease, maintenance, and return-condition analysis
- DOT fitness files
- FAA certificate history, manuals, OpSpecs, SMS records, and open compliance matters
- Airport, slot, gate, and route-authority transfer analysis
- Labor contracts, seniority, benefits, furlough, and training files
- Payment processor agreements, card holdbacks, refund liabilities, and loyalty-program documents
- Privacy policies and customer-data-transfer constraints
- Tax attribute study, including NOL preservation analysis
- ARTYMUS asset proof: wallet histories, smart-contract code, legal ownership chain, appraisal reports, audit reports, legal opinions, and cash-conversion mechanics

## Risk Matrix Summary

| Risk | Severity | Primary mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| ARTYMUS claims are not bankable | Critical | Independent forensic audit, valuation, legal opinions, cash-conversion waterfall, sponsor backstop |
| Authority-transfer delay | Critical | File early, preserve U.S. citizen control, prepare substitute certification path |
| Asset package overstates transferability | High | Build bid around confirmed assignable assets only |
| Insufficient restart liquidity | High | Oversize day-one liquidity and line up debt and equity simultaneously |
| Token structure creates regulatory exposure | High | Treat AADST as security; keep SRT non-investment and closed-loop initially |
| Foreign-ownership breach | High | Make offshore tranches non-voting and keep U.S. voting control |
| Labor restart friction | High | Labor diligence at signing and phased network relaunch |
| Antitrust / airport concentration issues | Medium-High | Early HSR and competition memo |
| Data/privacy transfer obstacles | Medium | Privacy review and court approval process if needed |

## Bottom-Line Recommendation

The legally coherent route is:

```text
Pursue a Section 363 stalking-horse transaction for selected assets.
Fund primarily with verified sponsor and institutional capital.
Use AADST only as a regulated securities layer.
Launch SRT only after operational relaunch.
Do not pursue minority-stock or creeping-control strategies.
```

That is the route most likely to be lawful, credible to creditors and regulators, and executable.

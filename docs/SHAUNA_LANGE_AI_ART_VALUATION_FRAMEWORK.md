## Executive Positioning

Shauna Lee Lange’s valuation framework reframes art valuation as a structured intelligence problem.

The market is no longer only asking:

> Who made it?

It is increasingly asking:

> Can we prove it, model it, price its risk, and predict its future market behavior?

This framework treats an artwork as an asset with measurable layers:

- Authorship
- Provenance
- Condition
- Liquidity
- Geography
- Timing
- Appraiser context
- Cultural narrative
- Digital visibility
- Collector network behavior

The core shift:

> Art valuation is moving from expert narrative into structured intelligence.

## Core Equation

```text
Artwork Value = Authorship × Provenance × Condition × Liquidity × Timing × Geography × Narrative
                minus Risk, Friction, Uncertainty, and Drift
```

In Reactor Theory / RT11 language:

```text
Valuation Coherence = (Identity × Evidence × Demand × Control) / Drift
```

Where:

- **Identity** = artist, authorship, career stage, institutional position
- **Evidence** = provenance, documentation, title, authenticity
- **Demand** = liquidity, collector depth, market comparables
- **Control** = condition, legal clarity, appraiser discipline, sale strategy
- **Drift** = uncertainty, gaps, volatility, fraud risk, poor timing, weak market placement

## The Seven-Layer Valuation Stack

## 1. Authorship

Authorship remains the primary price engine.

Key parameters:

- Artist identity
- Career stage
- Known body of work
- Institutional validation
- Market history
- Gallery representation
- Collector network power
- Digital footprint

The next generation of authorship valuation is graph-based.

An artist’s identity becomes a network:

```text
Artist -> Galleries -> Collectors -> Museums -> Auctions -> Critics -> Media -> Market Confidence
```

---

## 2. Provenance

Provenance is moving from storytelling into structured data.

Key parameters:

- Ownership history
- Exhibition records
- Invoices
- Labels
- Publications
- Institutional records
- Legal title
- Restitution risk
- Documentary gaps

Shauna’s provenance hierarchy treats evidence by evidentiary strength, from direct artist authentication and origin records down to weak signals such as hearsay, oral history, and unsupported statements.

The rule:

> The closer the document is to origin, the stronger the valuation support.

---

## 3. Condition

Condition operates as both physical reality and financial discount rate.

Key parameters:

- Surface stability
- Paint adhesion
- Craquelure
- Flaking
- Canvas or support integrity
- Restoration history
- Conservation documentation
- Environmental exposure
- Visual coherence

Condition is not just descriptive anymore.

AI imaging, spectral analysis, and degradation forecasting can turn conservation language into risk-adjusted price modeling.

---

## 4. Liquidity

Liquidity determines how reliably an artwork can become cash again.

Key parameters:

- Transaction frequency
- Sell-through rate
- Buyer pool depth
- Price variance
- Gallery support
- Institutional absorption
- Time-to-sale
- Comparable market activity

The art market behaves like finance here.

A thin market artist may require a 20–40% liquidity discount. A deep market artist may carry little discount or even a premium.

---

## 5. Geography

The same artwork can have different values in New York, London, Hong Kong, Paris, Seoul, or private advisory channels.

Key parameters:

- City-specific comparables
- Regional collector demand
- Currency and tax environment
- Auction house placement strategy
- Capital flow
- Buyer identity distribution
- Import/export constraints

The painting does not have one price.

It has a price map.

---

## 6. Timing

Timing can move value materially.

Key parameters:

- Market cycle
- Auction seasonality
- Interest rates
- Equity market confidence
- Collector sentiment
- Inventory pressure
- Exhibition momentum
- Media visibility

A strong work placed in the wrong cycle can underperform.

A well-positioned work during a peak collector window can outperform materially.

---

## 7. Professional Appraiser Context

Valuation purpose determines valuation output.

The same artwork may have different values depending on whether the context is:

- Fair market value
- Insurance replacement value
- Estate / tax valuation
- Lending collateral value
- Liquidation value
- Private sale estimate
- Auction estimate

Without defining the purpose, the number is unstable.
